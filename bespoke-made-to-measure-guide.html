Bespoke vs Made-to-Measure vs Off-the-Rack

The three words get used loosely, but they describe very different ways of making a suit. Knowing the difference helps you choose, and spend, wisely.

05101520253035404550556065707580859095100105110115120

AOff-the-rack

Ready-made suits are cut to standard sizes and sold finished. They are the most affordable and you can wear them today. Most people need small alterations, usually to the sleeves and trouser hems, to make an off-the-rack suit look its best.

BMade-to-measure

A standard pattern is adjusted to your measurements, then made in a workroom.

  • You choose cloth, lapels, buttons and lining.
  • Usually one fitting, sometimes none.
  • Fits better than ready-made, costs less than bespoke.

CBespoke

A paper pattern is drafted from scratch for your body alone. The suit is cut, basted together and refined across several fittings. Much of the shaping is done by hand, including the canvas inside the chest that lets the coat mould to you over time.

DWhat you pay for

The price difference reflects hours of skilled work, the number of fittings and the construction inside the jacket. A fully canvassed chest, hand-padded lapels and hand-sewn buttonholes all take time, and they make a suit last and drape better.

EWhich to choose

If you have standard proportions and a modest budget, a good ready-made suit with careful alterations works well. If you have a harder-to-fit figure or want specific details, made-to-measure is a smart step. Bespoke suits those who want a suit to last a decade or more.
