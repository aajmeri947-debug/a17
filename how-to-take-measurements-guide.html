How to Take Your Measurements

Good measurements are the foundation of a good fit. With a soft tape and a friend to help, you can take them like a tailor in ten minutes.

05101520253035404550556065707580859095100105110115120

ABefore you start

Use a soft fabric tape, not a metal one. Wear a thin shirt and the trousers you usually wear. Stand naturally, breathe normally and keep the tape snug but not tight: you should be able to slide one finger underneath it.

BChest and waist

These two set your jacket size.

  • Chest: under the arms, around the fullest part, tape level all round.
  • Waist: where your trousers sit, not necessarily your natural waist.
  • The difference between them is called the drop.

CSeat and inseam

Measure the seat around the fullest part of the hips with feet together. For the inseam, measure from the crotch seam of well-fitting trousers down to where you want the hem to fall, usually with a slight break over the shoe.

DSleeve and shoulder

For sleeve length, measure from the shoulder edge down to the wrist bone with your arm relaxed by your side. Shoulder width is taken across the back from one shoulder point to the other. These are the trickiest, so ask a friend to help.

EWrite it down

Record each measurement twice and use the average. Note the date, since bodies change. Share these numbers with your tailor or a made-to-measure service, and use our jacket size finder to see where you fall on a standard chart.
